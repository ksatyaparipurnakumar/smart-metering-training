The big picture

Why the country is changing every consumer meter, and the four layers you will work across.

RDSS in five points

  • Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme of the Ministry of Power: total outlay ₹3,03,758 crore with Gross Budgetary Support of ₹97,631 crore.
  • Goal: bring AT&C losses down to 12–15% and close the gap between cost of supply and revenue realised (ACS–ARR).
  • About 22.23 crore prepaid smart meters sanctioned for consumers, DTs and feeders (check current MoP figures before quoting).
  • Delivered through AMISPs (AMI Service Providers) on the DBFOOT model: Design, Build, Finance, Own, Operate, Transfer. The utility pays on performance.
  • Prepaid logic lives centrally in the MDMS and billing system, not on a smart card in the meter.

Four layers of AMI

LayerWhat it containsWho you call
1 · Physical edgeIS 16444 meter, CT/PT wiring, terminal block, sealsField crew, AMISP installer
2 · TransportCellular 4G / NB-IoT, RF mesh + DCU, PLCAMISP network team, telecom
3 · Data engineHES (DLMS/COSEM, IS 15959), MDMS with VEEAMISP IT, utility IT
4 · OperationsPrepaid billing, DT energy accounting, GIS, OMS, SCADARevenue, energy audit, O&M
Remember: a communicating meter is not automatically a billing-ready meter. Raw telemetry in the HES must pass MDMS validation before it becomes billable.

The four data profiles in memory

  1. Instantaneous: live V, I, kW. Read on demand by the HES.
  2. Load profile: 15/30-minute blocks. Stored locally typical 35–60 days during network outages.
  3. Billing profile: midnight snapshot of cumulative registers (kWh, kVAh, MD). The MDMS bills from this.
  4. Event / tamper profile: permanent, time-stamped log of anomalies such as cover open or neutral disturbance.

Installation and commissioning checklist

  1. De-energise and isolate. Prove zero potential on all phases and neutral.
  2. Mount vertically (within ±5°). Phase association: R-in to R-out.
  3. Torque terminals to 2.5–3.0 Nm. Loose screws cause hot spots and fires.
  4. For CT meters, check P1 faces the source and S1/S2 go to the meter's I-in / I-out. Earth S2 at one point only.
  5. Power on. Network LED goes from blinking to steady. RSSI better than −95 dBm.
  6. Scan MAC / ICCID / meter serial number in the AMISP app. Run the ping test.
  7. Confirm the first index in HES matches the LCD and the asset shows Active in MDMS. Apply serialised seals and photograph.
Rule: a meter without a digital commissioning ping does not exist in the utility's system.

How the reading leaves the meter, how the Head-End System manages it, and how the MDMS decides whether it can be billed.

What the HES does

  • Onboarding: registers each meter by serial number, MAC and SIM ICCID.
  • Passive reads: instantaneous values on demand; scheduled load, billing and event profiles.
  • Active commands: relay disconnect/reconnect SLA typical <10 min, time sync, parameter changes, firmware over the air (signed, encrypted).
  • Health: last communication time, RSSI, command success rate.

What the MDMS does

  • Collects raw DLMS payloads, removes duplicates, flags missing blocks and bad CRC.
  • Converts data to a standard model (CIM) and masks consumer identity for DPDP Act compliance.
  • Runs VEE: Validation, Estimation, Editing. Only passed data reaches billing.
  • Holds the master repository for load profiles, billing data and analytics.
Clock rules: the HES corrects meter clocks when drift crosses a small set limit (the decks show ±5 min). The MDMS rejects interval data when drift is larger, typical 30 min. Confirm both limits in your AMISP contract.

03

Prepaid engine & consumer complaints

How the daily ledger works, the words consumers will use with you, and six complaint cases with their real causes.

Postpaid vs prepaid

  • Postpaid: consume → monthly reading → bill weeks later → arrears → manual disconnection.
  • Prepaid: recharge first → 15-min tracking → midnight ledger deduction → low-balance alert → remote disconnect at zero → automatic reconnect on recharge.

The nine-step prepaid cycle

  1. Consumer recharges through app / UPI.
  2. Billing system updates the ledger.
  3. Meter records 15-min kWh blocks.
  4. Cost = kWh × ToD rate + daily share of fixed charges.
  5. Deduction posted at midnight.
  6. Balance crosses the alert level (e.g. ₹100).
  7. SMS and app alert sent.
  8. At ₹0 the MDMS asks the HES to open the relay, except in notified non-disconnection hours (nights, holidays).
  9. On recharge the HES closes the relay; the consumer may need to press the meter button to confirm.

Billing words to explain to consumers

  • kWh active energy (OBIS 1.8.0); kVAh apparent energy, billed to commercial/industrial to encourage good power factor.
  • Maximum demand: highest average load in any 15/30-min block of the billing cycle.
  • ToD tariff: different rates by time of day, as notified in the APERC tariff order.
  • Fixed charges are spread daily; adjustments include arrears instalments and subsidy credits.

Energy accounting, tamper & solar

Using interval data to find losses, reading tamper events correctly, and getting net metering right.

The energy waterfall

Feeder meter (Class 0.2S, 11 kV) → DT meter (Class 0.5S) → consumer meters (Class 1.0). Accounting only works when all three tiers report time-synchronised interval data.

What a DT loss figure proves

  • Proves: energy is leaving the network unbilled.
  • Does not prove: theft. Causes can be technical (heating, undersized conductors), commercial (consumers mapped to the wrong DT), hardware (DT meter CT wiring) or theft.

Case: the ghost feeder

An 11 kV feeder's loss jumped from 11.2% to 28.4%. Twelve DTs showed 45–60% loss. GIS cross-check found 340 consumers tagged to the wrong DT during app commissioning. After correcting the hierarchy in MDMS the feeder loss came to 9.8%, with no truck roll.

  • Import (grid → home, +P) is quadrants 1 and 4 and adds to 1.8.0. Export (home → grid, −P) is quadrants 2 and 3 and adds to 2.8.0.
  • With S1/S2 reversed the meter sees the opposite direction: export is recorded as import and import as export. A negative power factor at midday is the giveaway.
  • Before calling it a fault, check the operating condition, the meter data, CT/PT connections and phase association together.

Grid integration: the autonomous outage

  1. T+0: a storm drops an LT line.
  2. T+1 min: 45 meters push "Power Failure" to HES on their backup power.
  3. T+3 min: MDMS correlates the simultaneous events.
  4. T+5 min: GIS confirms all 45 share one DT.
  5. T+10 min: OMS dispatches the crew before the first consumer calls.

Keeping the system secure, a disciplined triage habit, the replacement SOP, holding the AMISP to its contract, and the capstone exercise.

Triage: never jump to conclusions, trace the data

  1. Field / wiring: tampering, neutral bypass, consumer side fault?
  2. Meter hardware: blank display, welded relay?
  3. Communication: SIM, RF node, APN?
  4. HES: command timeout, decryption failure, HES down?
  5. MDMS / billing: VEE rejection, interface failure?

Meter replacement SOP

  1. Diagnose: confirm stoppage, erratic data or display failure.
  2. Isolate: open upstream cut-outs, prove dead on phases and neutral.
  3. Replace: fit the new meter, torque to 2.5–3.0 Nm.
  4. Commission: network latch, sync device ID in the AMISP app.
  5. Document: photograph old final index, seal, match physical and digital readings.

Twenty field rules

  1. An event log is an anomaly to investigate, not a penalty.
  2. Zero consumption with good communication: suspect neutral / earth or phase bypass.
  3. Solar export errors: check S1/S2 first.
  4. Triage in order: Field → Meter → Comms → HES → MDMS → Billing.
  5. Never replace a meter without isolating upstream.
  6. Torque matters: 2.5–3.0 Nm.
  7. Cover-open records are permanent, even without mains.
  8. Photograph the final index before removal.
  9. Match the class: 1.0 domestic, 0.5S CT commercial, 0.2S feeders.
  10. Standardise phase allocation at the terminal block.
  1. Sudden feeder loss spikes usually mean GIS mapping errors.
  2. RSSI worse than −105 dBm: fit a high-gain external antenna.
  3. Meters keep weeks of profile data through outages; request backfill.
  4. Prepaid disconnection is decided by MDMS logic, not the meter.
  5. Clock drift ruins data: keep NTP sync healthy.
  6. Failed remote disconnect: suspect a welded relay or low auxiliary voltage.
  7. Enforce the 98% data availability clause.
  8. Escalate urban non-communicators after 48 hours.
  9. Hands off the internals: use optical probe and buttons only.
  10. The meter is a sensor; validated MDMS data is the product.
